W.i.s.e Values Simply

30–45 min

Three multicultural children's stories bring the w.i.s.e values to life in ways that are simple to understand and easy to remember. Each story runs five to ten minutes. Between the stories, we share a little about each principle, how we live it: in how we communicate with each other, in how we relate to the Earth, and ultimately in how we relate to ourselves and the work we are here to do in the world. We also, depending on the time length requested, can facilitate interactive practices to further integrate the ideas.


Wellness: a Eastern European rural story who moral is in recognizing that wellness is a choice we can make in any moment.
We then engage the interactive practice: The Well Breath: Together we discover a simple shift in the way we breathe, one that turns an ordinary breath into a deep choice for wellness. Then we feel what happens when we realize that choice is always within reach.

Interconnection: a Afro-Indigenous story that anchors the ways everything is connected, even when we would rather not acknowledge it.
We then engage the interactive practice: Hidden Threads: We take a quiet moment to reflect, and perhaps write down, one connection in our lives we've been overlooking: something we impact, or that impacts us, in a way that makes our wellness harder to reach. Then, as a few of us share, we explore together what happens when we tend that thread in a new way, one that amplifies our felt sense of wellness.

Sovereignty: a Southern Black folkstory of how the choice to anchor into our wellness is always ours, no matter what the outside world presents.
We then engage the interactive practice: Hold Your Center: In pairs, one partner does everything they can to make the other laugh, with silly faces, jokes, and funny stories, while the other stays centered within themselves. Then we feel what happens when we remember that anything coming from outside us, whether laughter, fear, or anger, is ours to choose to engage or let pass.

Playful enough for the youngest listeners, with roots deep enough for our elders.

Capoeira Angola

An introduction to Capoeira Angola through its history, music, and movement – a living tradition carried for generations.


What other art form or practice do you know of that combines martial art, dance, music, poetry, healing, ritual, folklore, acrobatics, improvisation, strategy, bio-mimicry, intercultural connection, and spirituality; and is even inclusive to anyone regardless of age, ethnicity, religion, or identity?! This is the beauty of Capoeira Angola, the Afro-Brazilian freedom fighting art form that was and is still used to this day to liberate and empower the bodies, minds, spirits, and communities of its practitioners from oppression and struggle. You will often see its practitioners full of joy, smiles, and laughter, and among them, the art is called a "jogo" or game, where Capoeira is played.


Lev has been a devoted practitioner of the art for over a decade primarily as a student of Mestre Cobra Mansa, as well as other teachers along the way most notably Ras Tre Subira, Mastre Skher Brown, and ContraMestre Stefan Bonitz. During that time, Lev has taught and worked with Baltimore City Youth, performed with the Dancing Warriors, a Capoeira Edu-tainment Group at Baltimore City Schools, and is raising his own children with Capoeira Angola as a way of life. Workshops are customized to the group, taking age and experience level into account and invite the participants in to a new world in which they can explore a new way of moving their bodies, connecting with friends, and a cultural richness that transcends language barriers and body types.

Book a Capoeira Angola Experience for a memorable one of a kind taste of this rich cultural art that will leave you feeling connected, empowered, and enlivened to yourselves, to each other, and to nature itself.

Earth Skills

Natural incense harvesting and bundling: to walk around the forest and not only see what can be used for food or tools, but to see the potential of something to be burned – and to find plants you can turn into cordage to bundle your incense. From the moments where there's a smell we don't like, to the moments we want to bring in a smell we do – the smoke is a powerful tool, and knowing how to make fragrant wreaths from the Earth around you is a really powerful skill to have.

Palmetto weaving: a skill you can find in many situations, not just with palmetto fronds but with any plant that grows a certain way – weaving baskets, hats, and mats.

Wild herb foraging walks: we love to learn the land, the green allies that nourish us and give us what we need throughout the day – an experiential walk discovering what's in the environment we're sharing, even using some of these herbs in teas and meals in the moment.

Through many hard lessons and some deeply celebrated successes, we have realized how to begin transitioning from toxic-laden fluoride and misunderstanding around nutrition to re-mineralizing our teeth in simple ways with tools we can have on hand. W.i.s.e Communications

From who's right to what's whole

As a species, our deepest problems are not technical. We already have the solutions for all of us to live in w.i.s.dom (wellness, interconnection, and sovereignty); for us all to live in symbiotic abundance with the Earth. In that world, everyone could have access to what they need and symbiotically want, free of debt and free of coercion. What holds us back is how hard it can be to work together toward the more beautiful world we know is possible. This offering is about that missing piece: how we speak, listen, and co-create together.


How we got here

We look at a bit of our journey as a species, a soft overarching pattern. We trace how we came to spend so much energy arguing over who is right and who is wrong, who knows what is good and who knows what is bad.


The next step

We conscoiusly step out of this arguing over abstractions together and into our capacity of speaking from wholeness and inner guidance, oriented by a felt sense of wellness in the body. We soften our definitions, honoring that each of us has the sovereign capacity to define things differently. While also activating our exploration of holding this wellness orientation and sovereign capacities while also acknowledging the ways we ceaselessly impact each other. We explore all of this through the lens of the foundational values of w.i.s.dom. (wellness, interconnection, and sovereignty)

W.i.s.dom in action

We reflect on power-over dynamics and the roles of victim, villain, and vindicator. Then we explore power-with dynamics, which honor the w.i.s.e, creative source spark within each of us that moves either through us, as us, or by us. From there we hold space for folks to optionally share any conflicts they've had recently and we reflect together how it can be analyzed in the power-over context and transformed in the power-with one.


In our family

We also share how we apply these techniques at home, in our relational dynamics as a couple and with our children. We offer some of the ways we engage with our children from this alternative approach: asking how they define things rather than defining things for them, honoring all of our sovereignty and seeking and offering requests instead of demands, and meeting conflict from power-with rather than power-over. And we share the results we've observed along the way, as our children grow in their own w.i.s.dom and in their capacity to communicate it with others.


The culmination: The Wellspring Relay

A relay race with a twist. Each person moves at whatever pace feels well for them in that moment, whether walking, skipping, running, even crawling. The only 3 rules are for the mover is once they establish their gait they need to try and keep it for their entire section until they pass the token to the next teammate - who can only begin once it's in their hand. The second rule is that the next teammate can't do the same gait as the person before them. The third rule is that teammates must cheer each other on in a new way: instead of urging each other faster, we reflect what we see, like "Wow, I see you moving in this way!" "Is that what your wellness looks like?" And at the end comes a reflection on each participants experiences in the different roles in relationship to themselves and their teammates and what it really means to win.

Wellspring Infrastructure

An infrastructure we utilize to support the wellness of our body, both internally and externally, and also to clean our spaces.

We believe it is the evolution of a relationship with water. Just as we have gathered around water since the beginning of our journey on this planet, we believe this tool could be pivotal in reclaiming those gathering spaces – and supporting each other to recognize the ways we can impact each other's lives to save money, become more resourceful, and ultimately heal this planet.

Freeze Dryer

A piece of infrastructure that has been really helpful for feeling a deep sense of security in times when we really don't know what could happen in the future in relationship to the food we have. Whenever we experience abundance, we do everything we can to make that abundance last – and this is a tool that can make it last the longest and maintain the most nutritional value. We recommend it, have utilized it immensely, and intend to continue to do so.

Analema Water Wand

A tool at the leading edge of the evolving science of studying water. Very simple, and much more accessible than our wellspring infrastructure – and at the same time a very profound technology that can help us reclaim some of the deepest qualities of water in our life, by something so simple as a wand placed in the water.

When we began to understand the ways that water is impacted by our thoughts and actions – and by the chemicals and aggression toward this planet – we realized how important it is to reclaim the wisdom and intelligence that is always accessible in water. This wand brings the strongest energy possible into the water through their wonderful year-long technique of seeding the water with the beneficial energies of the Earth. In this way we can reclaim our waters not only structurally, but energetically as well.

W.i.s.e Values Simply

30–45 min

Three multicultural children's stories bring the w.i.s.e values to life in ways that are simple to understand and easy to remember. Each story runs five to ten minutes. Between the stories, we share a little about each principle, how we live it: in how we communicate with each other, in how we relate to the Earth, and ultimately in how we relate to ourselves and the work we are here to do in the world. We also, depending on the time length requested, can facilitate interactive practices to further integrate the ideas.


Wellness: a Eastern European rural story who moral is in recognizing that wellness is a choice we can make in any moment.
We then engage the interactive practice: The Well Breath: Together we discover a simple shift in the way we breathe, one that turns an ordinary breath into a deep choice for wellness. Then we feel what happens when we realize that choice is always within reach.

Interconnection: a Afro-Indigenous story that anchors the ways everything is connected, even when we would rather not acknowledge it.
We then engage the interactive practice: Hidden Threads: We take a quiet moment to reflect, and perhaps write down, one connection in our lives we've been overlooking: something we impact, or that impacts us, in a way that makes our wellness harder to reach. Then, as a few of us share, we explore together what happens when we tend that thread in a new way, one that amplifies our felt sense of wellness.

Sovereignty: a Southern Black folkstory of how the choice to anchor into our wellness is always ours, no matter what the outside world presents.
We then engage the interactive practice: Hold Your Center: In pairs, one partner does everything they can to make the other laugh, with silly faces, jokes, and funny stories, while the other stays centered within themselves. Then we feel what happens when we remember that anything coming from outside us, whether laughter, fear, or anger, is ours to choose to engage or let pass.

Playful enough for the youngest listeners, with roots deep enough for our elders.

Capoeira Angola

An introduction to Capoeira Angola through its history, music, and movement – a living tradition carried for generations.


What other art form or practice do you know of that combines martial art, dance, music, poetry, healing, ritual, folklore, acrobatics, improvisation, strategy, bio-mimicry, intercultural connection, and spirituality; and is even inclusive to anyone regardless of age, ethnicity, religion, or identity?! This is the beauty of Capoeira Angola, the Afro-Brazilian freedom fighting art form that was and is still used to this day to liberate and empower the bodies, minds, spirits, and communities of its practitioners from oppression and struggle. You will often see its practitioners full of joy, smiles, and laughter, and among them, the art is called a "jogo" or game, where Capoeira is played.


Lev has been a devoted practitioner of the art for over a decade primarily as a student of Mestre Cobra Mansa, as well as other teachers along the way most notably Ras Tre Subira, Mastre Skher Brown, and ContraMestre Stefan Bonitz. During that time, Lev has taught and worked with Baltimore City Youth, performed with the Dancing Warriors, a Capoeira Edu-tainment Group at Baltimore City Schools, and is raising his own children with Capoeira Angola as a way of life. Workshops are customized to the group, taking age and experience level into account and invite the participants in to a new world in which they can explore a new way of moving their bodies, connecting with friends, and a cultural richness that transcends language barriers and body types.

Book a Capoeira Angola Experience for a memorable one of a kind taste of this rich cultural art that will leave you feeling connected, empowered, and enlivened to yourselves, to each other, and to nature itself.

Sustainable Access through Dynamic Reciprocity

10 min – 1 hour, interactive

What if access to what we need didn't depend on how much money we have, but on the agreements we make with each other? This offering explores how communities have always created abundance together, and how we can do it again today, in ways that fit our lives and our gatherings.


The story: Magic Coin Soup

We begin with a short story rooted in the Mijikenda people of Kenya, about a community that discovers how much becomes possible when each person brings what they can to the pot.


The roots: commitment pooling

From there, we explore the history of commitment pooling: credit and debt systems built on the agreements of the community itself. Instead of bartering one-on-one, people exchange with the group. And what they bring can be physical goods in the moment, skills and services, or promises for the future, so needs can be met from many different angles.


In the real world

In the longer version of this offering, we walk through ways this is already happening today:

Community currencies: using cryptocurrency and digital ledgers to hold a community's commitments in a way that's transparent and shared.

Cosmo Local mapping: a simple map of the offers people have in an area, so neighbors can see at a glance what's available and who to connect with.

Event-based credit systems: organizing around a single event, making its funds transparent, and making it clear what's needed, what's being asked for, and what each person has contributed, so everyone can find their own way to access the gathering.


Brainstorm together

With time allowing, we brainstorm with a few participants on how these approaches could best serve their own communities or events, turning the ideas into first steps they can take home.


Catered to any age range, from a simple story for families to a deep-dive for organizers and event hosts.

W.i.s.e Communications

From who's right to what's whole

As a species, our deepest problems are not technical. We already have the solutions for all of us to live in w.i.s.dom (wellness, interconnection, and sovereignty); for us all to live in symbiotic abundance with the Earth. In that world, everyone could have access to what they need and symbiotically want, free of debt and free of coercion. What holds us back is how hard it can be to work together toward the more beautiful world we know is possible. This offering is about that missing piece: how we speak, listen, and co-create together.


How we got here

We look at a bit of our journey as a species, a soft overarching pattern based around Riane Eisler's the Chalice and the Blade . We trace how we came to spend so much energy arguing over who is right and who is wrong as if any of us have the "objectively true" definition for any abstraction.


The next step

We consciously step out of this arguing over abstractions together and into our capacity of speaking from wholeness and inner guidance, oriented by a felt sense of wellness in the body. We soften our definitions, honoring that each of us has the sovereign capacity to define things differently. While also activating our exploration of holding this wellness orientation and sovereign capacities while also acknowledging the ways we ceaselessly impact each other. We explore all of this through the lens of the foundational values of w.i.s.dom. (wellness, interconnection, and sovereignty)

W.i.s.dom in action

We reflect on power-over dynamics and the roles of victim, villain, and vindicator. Then we explore power-with dynamics, which honor the w.i.s.e, creative source spark within each of us that moves either through us, as us, or by us. From there we hold space for folks to optionally share any conflicts they've had recently and we reflect together how it can be analyzed in the power-over context and transformed in the power-with one.


In our family

We also share how we apply these techniques at home, in our relational dynamics as a couple and with our children. We offer some of the ways we attempt to engage with our children from this alternative approach: asking how they define things rather than defining things for them, honoring all of our sovereignty and seeking and offering requests instead of demands, and meeting conflict from power-with rather than power-over. And we share the results we've observed along the way, as we and our children grow in their own w.i.s.dom and in all of our capacity to communicate it with others.


The culmination: The Wellspring Relay

A relay race with a twist. Each person moves at whatever pace feels well for them in that moment, whether walking, skipping, running, even crawling. The only 3 rules are: 1, once the mover establishes his or her gait she or he needs to try and keep it for the entire section until he or she passes the token to the next teammate - who can only begin once it's in his or her hand. The second rule is that the next teammate can't do the same gait as the person before him or her. The third rule is that teammates must cheer each other on in a new way: instead of urging each other faster, we reflect what we see, like "Wow, I see you moving in this way!" "Is that what your wellness looks like?" And at the end comes a reflection on each participants experiences in the different roles in relationship to themselves and their teammates and what it really means to win.
